# COLD OPEN

How do you build a city eighty-five metres down, into solid rock, with nothing but hand tools? And why would anyone want to? Beneath an ordinary town in central Turkey there is a second town. It was not built. It was dug, straight down into the rock, level below level. It had stables, kitchens, wine presses, wells and a church, and doors of solid stone that could be rolled shut from the inside. This is Derinkuyu. In this film we answer three questions: how it was built, who built it, and why people would choose to live underground.

For centuries the town above had almost forgotten it. Then, in 1963, a man renovating his house knocked through a wall and found a passage behind it. The passage led to another, and that one led down. What the people of Derinkuyu uncovered over the following years was one of the largest underground settlements ever found.

It was not alone. Across the region of Cappadocia, dozens of other underground settlements have been found, some small, some huge, a few joined, perhaps, by tunnels. Together they are among the strangest things that people have ever made: whole villages that could vanish into the ground.

But almost everything you may have heard about it needs checking. That it has eighteen levels. That twenty thousand people lived in it. That a Greek soldier described it two and a half thousand years ago. Some of this is true, some is exaggerated, and some is simply wrong. Let us go down and see what the evidence actually says.
