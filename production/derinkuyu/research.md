## Claims checked (first pass: Wikipedia and its cited sources; confirm with scholarly sources before scripting)
- Depth about 85 m: Wikipedia lead, no inline citation. Widely given; keep as "about 85 metres".
- Levels: **"18 levels" is not supported.** Wikipedia mentions a church on "the lowest (fifth) level" (BBC Travel, Truman 2022). Do not state a total until a scholarly source confirms one.
- Capacity "20,000": cited only to media (BBC Travel 2022, National Geographic 2015). No named researcher. Narration: "estimates run as high as 20,000 people".
- Phrygians, 8th–7th c. BC: Wikipedia hedges ("might"), citing a travel site. Narration: "some scholars think the first chambers may be Phrygian". Needs a better source.
- Xenophon, *Anabasis* 4.5.25 describes underground houses in **Armenia**, not Cappadocia. Often misused for Derinkuyu. If used, say so accurately.
- Byzantine enlargement during the Arab–Byzantine wars (c. 780–1180): Wikipedia cites History.com. Confirm with Byzantine scholarship (e.g. Ousterhout, *Visualizing Community: Art, Material Culture, and Settlement in Byzantine Cappadocia*, 2017).
- 1963 rediscovery: a resident renovating his house knocked through a wall (History.com 2016). No name is given; do not invent one.
- More than 600 entrances: BBC Travel 2022.
- Rolling stone doors, closable from inside floor by floor. Size and weight need a source.
- 55 m well shaft (Phys.org / Acoustical Society of America 2025). Central shaft about 3 × 2.8 m (Aydan & Ulusay 2013).
- 8–9 km tunnel to Kaymaklı (Martin 2017, *The Evolution Underground*). Treat as "reported"; confirm.
- Used by Cappadocian Greeks and Armenians into the 20th century (Dawkins 1916). Abandoned after the 1923 population exchange (Darke 2011; Rodley 2010).
- UNESCO: Göreme National Park and the Rock Sites of Cappadocia, 1985, ref. 357.

## Second pass (2026-11-27, web search; Springer and the Turkish Ministry sites are blocked from this environment)
- Levels: Wikipedia says "at least eight levels"; the site's official visitor page says eight levels are open today and the District Governorship estimates 12–13 once fully cleared. **Narration: "at least eight levels are known".** "18" stays out.
- Depth: about 85 m is consistent across sources. Keep "about 85 metres".
- Rolling stones: published sizes and weights conflict (200 kg, 500 kg, "up to 1.5 m"), none from a survey. **Narration gives no weight; it says "round stone doors, rolled across the passage from the inside".**
- Engineering source to cite: Aydan Ö. & Ulusay R. (2013), *Geomechanical evaluation of Derinkuyu antique underground city and its implications in geoengineering*, Rock Mechanics and Rock Engineering 46: 731–754. A secondary summary credits it with: tuff uniaxial compressive strength of roughly 1–10 MPa; monitoring on the fifth and seventh floors since 1996; wells dug downward, air shafts upward. Confirm against the paper before any of these goes on screen.
- Ousterhout (2017) on Byzantine Cappadocian settlement: not reachable here; the Byzantine-refuge framing stays hedged ("greatly enlarged in the middle Byzantine period, when Arab raids reached Cappadocia").
