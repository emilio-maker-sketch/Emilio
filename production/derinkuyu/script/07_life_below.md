# LIFE BELOW

What was it like to shelter here? No one who did so left us a description, so we have to read the rooms themselves, and be honest about where the evidence stops.
> WS | slow push | photo | neutral | | Dim chamber in Derinkuyu [q: Derinkuyu Underground City 9789 Nevit]

The first thing to understand is the darkness. Below the first levels no daylight reaches at all. Every task, cooking, feeding animals, caring for the sick, was done by the light of small oil lamps, and the niches cut for them can still be seen in the walls, often with soot on the rock above. Lamps burn oil and use up air, which is one more reason the ventilation mattered so much.

> MS | drift | photo | neutral | | Dark corridor lit by a single light [q: Derinkuyu Underground City 9925 Nevit]

The second is the cold. The underground keeps a cool, steady temperature, which is excellent for storing food and wine, and miserable for sitting still for days. People would have brought blankets and bedding down with them, along with grain, oil, dried food and water jars. The many storerooms and niches show how much of the city was simply a larder.
> WS | slow pan | photo | neutral | | Storage room with niches in Derinkuyu [q: Derinkuyu Underground City 9813 Nevit]
> STAT | static | stat | neutral | | cool all year :: ideal for storage, hard for people

The third is the animals. The stables near the top were not decoration. A family that left its sheep, goats and draught animals above ground would lose them to the raiders, and with them its future. So the animals came down too, which means the smell, the noise and the waste of the animals came too.
> WS | slow push | photo | neutral | | Stable with carved troughs near the top of Derinkuyu [q: Derinkuyu Underground City stable]

How long could people stay? The honest answer is that we do not know. With stored food and a well, a community could in principle hold out for weeks. Most raids, though, were measured in days. The point of the city was not to live below for ever. It was to outlast the danger and come back up to fields and houses that were still there.
