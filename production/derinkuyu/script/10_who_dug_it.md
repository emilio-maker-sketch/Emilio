# CHAPTER TEN: WHO DUG IT?

So who were they? This is the hardest question about Derinkuyu, and the honest answer is that no one knows who cut the first chamber. There is no inscription at the entrance and no founder's name. Carved rooms are very hard to date, because rock has no layers that build up over time the way a mound of earth does.

You will often read that it was begun by the Phrygians, a people of Anatolia in the eighth and seventh centuries before Christ. Some scholars have suggested this, and Phrygians did cut rock monuments elsewhere in Anatolia. But there is no direct evidence linking them to Derinkuyu. It is a possibility, not a fact.
> MAP | slow push | map | map | | Map of Anatolia: Phrygia in the west, Cappadocia in the centre

Another favourite claim is that the Greek writer Xenophon described Derinkuyu. Around 400 BC he marched across Anatolia with an army of Greek mercenaries, and in his book, the Anabasis, he describes villages whose houses were dug into the ground, entered through a narrow opening like the mouth of a well and wide inside, with ramps for the animals and ladders for the people.

> STAT | static | stat | neutral | | Anabasis 4.5.25 :: houses dug into the ground, entered through a narrow opening like a well

But read where he says it. Xenophon was describing villages in Armenia, in the mountains of eastern Anatolia, hundreds of kilometres from Cappadocia. His houses were real, and they show that living underground was an old idea in Anatolia. They are not Derinkuyu.

Others have pointed to the Hittites, or to people even older. Again there is no evidence. The Hittites did build underground passages, such as the long stone tunnel under the walls of Hattusa, but those were built of masonry, not cut into soft rock, and they are a long way from Derinkuyu.
> WS | slow push | photo | neutral | | The Yerkapı tunnel under the ramparts of Hattusa {LT: Yerkapı | Hittite tunnel, Hattusa} [q: Yerkapi tunnel Hattusa]

Most researchers think the city grew in stages. The upper levels and the lower ones are not necessarily the same age, and rooms were widened, joined and dug deeper over many centuries. Derinkuyu was not built. It accumulated.
