# Research: The Fall of Carthage

## Sources

### Ancient
| Source | Date | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Polybius, *Histories* (books 1–3, 6, 10, 15, 36–39) | c. 150–120 BC | The best source. A Greek hostage in Rome, friend of Scipio Aemilianus, and an eyewitness of the fall of Carthage in 146 BC. Talked to veterans of the Hannibalic war and read Hannibal's own bronze inscription at Cape Lacinium. Books on 146 survive only in fragments. |
| Livy, *History of Rome* books 21–30 | c. 25 BC–AD 10 | Full narrative of the Second Punic War. Patriotic, dramatic, often follows Polybius. Books on the Third War are lost (only summaries). |
| Appian, *Libyca* (Punic Wars) | c. AD 160 | Our only continuous narrative of the Third Punic War, 149–146 BC, largely based on Polybius. Numbers (armour, catapults, 50,000 survivors) come from here. |
| Plutarch, *Cato the Elder*, *Fabius Maximus* | c. AD 100 | The fig, "Carthage must be destroyed", Fabius the Delayer. |
| Diodorus Siculus | 1st century BC | Carthage and Sicily; tophet sacrifice in 310 BC. |
| Justin (epitome of Pompeius Trogus) | 2nd–3rd century AD? | The legend of Elissa (Dido). |
| Cornelius Nepos, *Hannibal* | 1st century BC | Hannibal's last years. |
| Aristotle, *Politics* book 2 | 4th century BC | Praises the Carthaginian constitution. |

Almost everything we know about Carthage was written by its enemies, Greeks and Romans. Carthaginian literature has not survived, apart from fragments of Mago's farming manual and inscriptions.

## Visual accuracy notes
- Mid-Republic Roman soldiers (3rd–2nd century BC): Montefortino bronze helmets, often with feather crests; bronze square chest plates or mail; oval curved shields; javelins (pila); short Spanish swords. No segmented plate armour, no rectangular imperial shields.
- Carthaginian armies were multinational: Libyan heavy infantry, Iberians (white tunics with crimson borders, curved falcata swords), Gauls, Balearic slingers, Numidian light cavalry riding without saddle or bridle.
- War elephants: North African forest elephants, smaller than Asian elephants. Whether they carried towers is doubtful; we show riders on the neck.
- Carthage: hill-top citadel (Byrsa), tall flat-roofed houses of five or six storeys, Hellenistic columns, circular naval harbour. No domes or arches.
- Every AI reconstruction is labelled in the description as an artistic interpretation.
