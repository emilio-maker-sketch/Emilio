# CHAPTER FOUR: THE OATH

Peace brought Carthage no rest. Its army in Sicily had been made up largely of mercenaries: Libyans, Iberians, Gauls, Greeks. Twenty thousand of them were shipped back to Africa expecting to be paid. The treasury was empty. The mercenaries mutinied, and the Libyan towns that Carthage had taxed so heavily rose with them.

What followed was so savage that Polybius called it the Truceless War. The rebels tortured and killed a Carthaginian envoy and seven hundred prisoners. Carthage answered in kind. Hamilcar Barca was given command, and after three years he trapped the main rebel army in a mountain valley known as the Saw, where the starving men, Polybius says, ate their prisoners and then their slaves before they surrendered.

The revolt nearly destroyed Carthage. And while it was helpless, Rome struck. In 238 BC, the Romans seized the island of Sardinia, and when Carthage protested, threatened war again unless it paid a further twelve hundred talents. Polybius, who admired Rome, admitted that this was without any justification. Many Carthaginians, Hamilcar among them, never forgot it.

In 237 BC, Hamilcar sailed for Spain with an army, to build Carthage a new empire there, rich in silver. Before he left, he made a sacrifice to the gods. His nine-year-old son begged to come with him. Hamilcar agreed, on one condition. He led the boy to the altar, made him place his hand on the sacrifice, and swear that he would never be a friend to Rome. The boy's name was Hannibal.

We know this story because Hannibal told it himself, many years later, to a foreign king. Whether every detail is true, we cannot say. But it is how he wanted to be understood.

In Spain, Hamilcar conquered and made alliances, and took over the rich silver mines of the south. When he drowned in a river during a battle in 229 BC, his son-in-law, Hasdrubal the Fair, took over. Hasdrubal founded a new capital on a superb natural harbour on the Spanish coast. He called it, too, Qart-hadasht. The Romans called it New Carthage. Today it is the Spanish city of Cartagena.

Rome, worried about this new power, made a treaty with Hasdrubal: the Carthaginians would not cross the river Ebro in arms. Then, in 221 BC, Hasdrubal was murdered by a Celt seeking revenge. The army in Spain chose a new commander by acclamation. He was twenty-six years old. It was Hamilcar's son. Hannibal.
