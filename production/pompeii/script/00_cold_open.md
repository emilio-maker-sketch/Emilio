# Cold Open

[v: Brullov Last Day of Pompeii]
[mood: dark]
It began, as so many catastrophes do, on an ordinary day.

[v: Pompeii street ; Via dell'Abbondanza Pompeii]
In the bakeries of Pompeii, the ovens had been lit before dawn. Loaves of bread, stamped with the baker's mark, were already cooling on the counters. In the street bars, clay jars set into stone counters held lentils, wine and hot food for the lunchtime crowd. On the walls, painted slogans urged citizens to vote for their favourite candidates in the coming elections.

[v: Pompeii fresco ; Pompeii peristyle garden]
In the great houses near the forum, slaves swept the courtyards and tended the gardens. Children learned their letters. Merchants haggled over the price of fish sauce and cloth. Somewhere, someone scratched a joke into the plaster of a wall, never imagining it would still be read two thousand years later.

[v: Mount Vesuvius from Pompeii ; Pompeii forum Vesuvius]
Above it all stood the mountain. Green with vineyards almost to its summit, it had been quiet for as long as anyone could remember. Nobody in Pompeii thought of it as a volcano. There was, in their language, barely a word for what it was about to become.

[v: Vesuvius eruption painting ; Volaire Vesuvius]
[mood: rising]
Shortly after midday, the mountain tore itself open.

[v: Plinian eruption column ; Volcanic ash cloud]
A column of ash and pumice rose more than thirty kilometres into the sky, higher than any storm cloud, and spread out across the heavens like the branches of a pine tree. Within an hour, stones were falling on Pompeii. By nightfall, the roofs were collapsing. By the following morning, a series of burning avalanches of gas and ash would sweep down the mountain at the speed of a hurricane, destroying everything in their path.

[v: Pompeii plaster casts victims ; Garden of the Fugitives Pompeii]
[mood: dark]
When it was over, Pompeii, Herculaneum, Stabiae and Oplontis had vanished. Thousands of people were dead. And for nearly seventeen centuries, the world would forget that these cities had ever existed.

[v: Pompeii ruins panorama ; Pompeii forum Vesuvius]
This is the story of the last days of Pompeii. Of the people who lived there, the warnings they could not read, the admiral who sailed into the disaster, and the young man who survived to tell the world what he saw. It is the story of how a city was erased in less than a day, and how, by destroying it, the volcano preserved it forever.
