# CHAPTER SIX: THE FIRST WEEKS

Under Islamic law and custom, a city was to be offered the chance to surrender before it was attacked. According to the chroniclers, Mehmed sent that offer at the start of the siege: the people's lives and property would be spared if the city opened its gates. The offer was refused. From that moment, if the city was taken by force, it could be given to the army.

Mehmed began by clearing the land around the city. A small fortress at Therapia, on the Bosphorus, held out for two days under cannon fire before its garrison surrendered. Another small fort near the village of Studios fell within hours. According to Kritoboulos, the men who survived were impaled in front of the walls. On the island of Prinkipo in the Sea of Marmara, the defenders of a tower refused to surrender, and the Ottoman fleet set it on fire. The message to the city was clear.

Inside the walls, the whole population was drawn into the defence. Men took their turns on the walls. Monks and priests carried stones. Women and children brought food and water to the fighters and helped to repair the damage. Constantine himself seems to have been everywhere, riding along the walls, inspecting the repairs, and spending the nights close to the most threatened sections.

In mid-April, the Ottoman fleet made its first attempt to break the chain and force its way into the Golden Horn. The Christian ships were waiting behind it. From their high decks, the crews fired arrows and stones down onto the smaller galleys, and the attack was driven back. It was a small victory, but it showed that the chain, and the ships that guarded it, could hold.

On the night of the eighteenth of April, Mehmed sent his troops against the damaged walls in the Lycus valley. The defenders held. The attackers were driven back after hours of fighting, with heavy losses. It was the first sign that this siege would not be quick.

For the first two weeks, the defence seemed to be working. The walls were being damaged, but they were being repaired. The enemy fleet had been held. And the defenders still believed that help from the West might arrive at any moment. They had no idea what Mehmed was planning next.
