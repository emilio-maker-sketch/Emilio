# Research notes and fact check

Every factual claim in the script is listed here with its basis and how confident we can be.
**Solid** means the main sources agree and modern historians accept it. **Reported** means it rests on one or two chroniclers and the script attributes it ("according to..."). **Disputed** means the script says the sources disagree.

## Primary sources used
| Source | Who | Bias to keep in mind |
|---|---|---|
| Nicolò Barbaro, *Diary of the Siege* | Venetian ship's doctor, in the harbour throughout | Precise on dates and harbour events; pro-Venetian, anti-Genoese |
| Leonard of Chios, letter to Pope Nicholas V (1453) | Catholic archbishop of Mytilene, in the city | Hostile to the anti-union Greeks |
| George Sphrantzes, *Chronicon Minus* | Constantine's friend and courtier | Personal and grieving. The long *Chronicon Maius* is a 16th-century expansion by Makarios Melissenos and is **not** treated as eyewitness |
| Doukas, *Historia* (c. 1460s) | Greek in Genoese service on Lesbos | Vivid; sole source for several famous stories (Kerkoporta, Notaras's turban line) |
| Kritoboulos of Imbros, *History of Mehmed the Conqueror* | Greek official under Mehmed | Written for Mehmed; flattering portrait |
| Laonikos Chalkokondyles, *Histories* | Athenian historian | Wrote later, at second hand |
| Tursun Beg, *Tarih-i Ebu'l-Feth* | Ottoman official present at the siege | Written decades later, panegyric |
| Nestor-Iskander, *Tale of Constantinople* | Slavonic account; claims service in the Ottoman army | Authenticity debated |

Modern syntheses checked against: Steven Runciman, *The Fall of Constantinople 1453* (1965); Donald Nicol, *The Immortal Emperor* (1992); Roger Crowley, *Constantinople: The Last Great Siege* (2005); Marios Philippides and Walter Hanak, *The Siege and the Fall of Constantinople in 1453* (2011).

## Visual accuracy rules (also in bible.json)
- Hagia Sophia in 1453 has **no minarets**. The first was added after the conquest.
- No Blue Mosque, Topkapı Palace, Galata Bridge or Süleymaniye in any 1453 shot.
- Janissaries: white felt *börk* caps. No fezzes (the fez is 19th century).
- Byzantine defenders: mixed, worn, practical kit. No uniform "Roman legion" look.
- No graphic violence or gore. Executions are seen only from a distance, in silhouette.
- Constantine's appearance is a reconstruction (no authentic portrait), and so is the 21-year-old Mehmed's (Bellini's portrait shows him at 48).
