### Judgement
1. **The biggest problem is the first minute, not the topics.** Half the audience leaves before 70 seconds. The curve is weak from the first second, which usually means the opening does not deliver what the title and thumbnail promised.
   Fix for every new film:
   - state the title's promise in the first 15 seconds
   - show the most striking real image early
   - keep the cold open under 60 seconds before the first payoff
2. **Search is the engine (65%), and underground places are what search finds us for.** Longyou, the Hypogeum, the Edinburgh vaults and the Egyptian labyrinth bring most of our search views. This is the clearest audience signal we have.
3. Titles built on "What X Actually Found in Egypt" outperform our average. "Not What You Were Told" should be used only when the film really overturns a belief.
4. The sleep / "fall asleep to" format underperforms on this channel (0.35×). Do not prioritise it.

  - *The Fall of Carthage: How Rome Erased Its Greatest Rival*: 91
  - *Hannibal, Scipio and the Fall of Carthage: The Full Story*: 89
- Recent competition: *The First Punic War* (History Mapped Out, 187K subscribers) 108K views; *Carthage Erased…* (Roman Docs) 3.5K views; *Why Rome's Enemies Feared Scipio Africanus…* 34K views.

### Judgement and recommendation
- **Title:** *Carthage Must Be Destroyed: The 118-Year War That Ended an Empire*. This is the top-scoring structure, corrected for accuracy: the Punic Wars ran 264–146 BC, which is 118 years, not 120. The "Carthage must be destroyed" line is Cato's, and the film covers it.
- **First line of the description:** "The fall of Carthage in 146 BC ended the Punic Wars, 118 years after they began: the story of Hannibal, Scipio Africanus and the city Rome swore to destroy."
- **Tags and chapters:** fall of Carthage, history of Carthage, Punic Wars, Hannibal Barca, Scipio Africanus, Cannae, Battle of Zama, Third Punic War. Chapter names should use those phrases where they fit the content.
- **Thumbnail concepts (1280 × 720):**
  1. The burning citadel of Byrsa at night, seen from the sea, one small Roman silhouette in the foreground. Text: **"146 BC"**.
  2. Split frame: Hannibal's war elephant on the left, the burning city on the right. Text: **"ERASED"**.
  3. A close-up of a Carthaginian coin (Tanit) cracked across, against a dark background. Text: **"DELETED"**. This uses a real artefact.
  - Recommendation: concept 1. It is the most readable at phone size, it is true to the film's climax, and it doesn't repeat the title.
- **Retention:** the Carthage cold open runs 1:47 before the title card. Given the 70-second drop-off seen above, the first 15 seconds must say plainly what the film is: Rome destroying Carthage in 146 BC, and why.
