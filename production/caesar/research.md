# Research: The Assassination of Julius Caesar

## Sources

### Ancient
| Source | Date | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Julius Caesar, *Commentaries* on the Gallic and Civil Wars | 50s–40s BC | Caesar's own account. Self-serving, but contemporary. |
| Cicero, letters and *Philippics* | 44–43 BC | The only eyewitness of the period whose own words survive. He was not told of the plot. |
| Nicolaus of Damascus, *Life of Augustus* | c. 20 BC onward | The earliest detailed narrative of the murder. Written for Augustus's circle. Gives 35 wounds. |
| Suetonius, *Divus Iulius* | c. AD 120 | Used the archives. Gives the omens, the 23 wounds, Antistius the doctor and the "kai su, teknon" report. |
| Plutarch, *Caesar*, *Brutus*, *Antony*, *Cicero* | c. AD 100–120 | Moral biographer with vivid detail. Main source for Porcia, Artemidorus and Popilius Laenas. |
| Appian, *Civil Wars* book 2 | c. AD 160 | Funeral speech, wax effigy. |
| Cassius Dio, *Roman History* book 44 | c. AD 220 | Senate debates, funeral. |

None of the ancient narratives of the murder is by an eyewitness. The fullest are written 150 or more years later.

### Modern
- Adrian Goldsworthy, *Caesar: Life of a Colossus* (2006)
- Barry Strauss, *The Death of Caesar* (2015). Stresses the role of Decimus Brutus.
- Tom Holland, *Rubicon* (2003)
- Mary Beard, *SPQR* (2015)
- Greg Woolf, *Et Tu, Brute? The Murder of Caesar and Political Assassination* (2006)

## Claim register

**Solid**: multiple sources or contemporary evidence. **Reported**: one later source or a tradition. **Disputed**: the sources disagree, or the claim is false.

| Claim | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Killed at a Senate meeting in the Curia of Pompey, 15 March 44 BC | Solid | Not the Capitol, not the Forum (Shakespeare). |
| About 60 or more conspirators | Reported | Suetonius: "more than sixty". |
| 23 wounds | Reported | Suetonius. Nicolaus of Damascus says 35. |
| Only one wound was fatal (the second, in the chest) | Reported | Suetonius, citing the physician Antistius. |
| "Et tu, Brute?" | False as history | Elizabethan stage line, made famous by Shakespeare (1599). |
| "Kai su, teknon?" ("You too, child?") | Disputed | Suetonius reports that "some" wrote this. He himself says Caesar made no sound but a groan. |
| "Beware the Ides of March" | Disputed in wording | Suetonius: Spurinna warned of danger lasting until the Ides. The exchange "they have come but not gone" is in both Suetonius and Plutarch. |
| Calpurnia's dream | Reported | Versions differ: holding his body (Plutarch), or the pediment of the house falling (Suetonius, Plutarch citing Livy). |
| Artemidorus's note | Reported | Plutarch. Suetonius says a note revealing the plot was found in his hand. |
| Antony kept outside the hall | Reported | Plutarch's *Brutus*: Trebonius. Plutarch's *Caesar*: Decimus. |
| Caesar fell at the base of Pompey's statue | Reported | Plutarch, Suetonius. |
| Three slaves carried the body home | Reported | Suetonius. |
| Will: Octavius adopted, gift of 300 sesterces per citizen, gardens left to the people | Solid | Suetonius, Plutarch (75 drachmae), Appian. |
| Decimus named as a reserve heir in the will | Reported | Suetonius, Appian. |
| Wax effigy with wounds at the funeral | Reported | Appian only. |
| Helvius Cinna killed in error | Solid | Plutarch, Suetonius, Appian, Dio. |
| The comet of July 44 BC | Solid | Pliny quotes Augustus's memoirs. |
| None of the assassins survived 3 years or died naturally | Reported, exaggerated | Suetonius. Several survived longer, e.g. Casca's fate is unclear. Say "most". |
| Crossing of the Rubicon, January 49 BC, "the die is cast" | Solid event, reported words | Suetonius: Latin. Plutarch: in Greek, quoting Menander. |
| Cleopatra smuggled in rolled in a carpet | Disputed | Plutarch: a bedding sack. The carpet is a later image. |
| Gallic War dead: 1,192,000 | Reported | Pliny the Elder, from Caesar's own count. Probably inflated. |
| "Veni, vidi, vici" | Solid | Suetonius (triumph placard), Plutarch (letter). |
| Caesar refused the diadem at the Lupercalia, 15 February 44 | Solid | Several sources. Motives disputed. |
| Planned to leave for the Parthian war on 18 March 44 | Solid | Appian. |
| Pirate ransom story (20 raised to 50 talents) | Reported | Plutarch and Suetonius. Details vary. |
| Brutus was Caesar's son | Rumour, unlikely | Caesar would have been about 15. |
| "The Republic is nothing, a mere name" | Reported | Suetonius, quoting the hostile writer Titus Ampius. |
| Cicero: the assassins had "the courage of men, the judgment of boys" | Solid | Cicero, letter to Atticus, 44 BC. |

## Visual accuracy rules
- **Legionaries of the late Republic:** mail shirts, bronze Montefortino or Coolus helmets, oval curved shields, pila. No segmented plate armour, which is later, and no rectangular imperial shields in 49 BC.
- **Senators:** white toga with a broad purple stripe on the tunic. The magistrates' toga has a purple border.
- **Roman men of 44 BC:** clean-shaven, with short hair.
- **The meeting hall:** the Curia of Pompey was a rectangular hall attached to the portico behind Pompey's theatre. Pompey's statue stood inside. It was not domed and not on a hill.
- **Writing:** wax tablets, styluses and papyrus rolls. No codex books and no paper.
- **No gore close-ups:** suggest violence through hands, togas, shadows and the reactions of the people present.
