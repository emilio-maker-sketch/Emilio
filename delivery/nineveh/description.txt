In 612 B.C., the Medes and Babylonians sacked Nineveh and set its palaces on fire. Inside was the library of King Ashurbanipal: tens of thousands of clay tablets. It is often said the fire baked them and saved them. The evidence tells a stranger story.

We follow the king who claimed he could read "stones from before the Flood", the library he built partly by confiscation, the siege recorded in the Babylonian Chronicle, the excavators who dug into the mound of Kuyunjik (one of them by night), the banknote engraver George Smith who found the flood story of Gilgamesh in a broken tablet in 1872, the destruction at Mosul and Nimrud in 2015-16, and the AI tool that is now rejoining the library's fragments.

NOTE ON VISUALS
The 3D tablet sequences and the silent-film scenes are illustrative reconstructions; the wedges on the 3D tablet are not a real text. The map shows rivers in their modern courses.
