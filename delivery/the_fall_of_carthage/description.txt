The fall of Carthage in 146 BC ended the Punic Wars, 118 years after they began: the story of Hannibal, Scipio Africanus and the city Rome swore to destroy.

In the spring of 146 BC, Roman soldiers fought their way, house by house, up the streets of Carthage. When it was over, the greatest city of the western Mediterranean was a ruin, and its people were sold as slaves.

This documentary tells the whole story: the Phoenician city founded from Tyre, its harbours and its empire, the wars with Rome over Sicily, Hannibal's oath, his march over the Alps and his victories at the Trebia, Lake Trasimene and Cannae, Scipio's answer at Zama, and the half-century of peace that ended with Cato's demand that Carthage must be destroyed. It separates the sources from the legends: no Romans sowing the ground with salt, and no certain portrait of Hannibal.
