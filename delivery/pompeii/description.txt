In 2024, geneticists pulled DNA from the bones inside Pompeii's famous plaster casts, and the stories we had told about those people for 150 years turned out to be wrong. The "mother" holding a child was a man, and he wasn't related to the child.

So what really happened on Pompeii's last day? Not the legend: the evidence. We follow the eruption hour by hour through the eyes of the only eyewitness, the 17-year-old Pliny the Younger. We watch the column rise, the pumice fall for 18 hours, the column collapse after midnight, and the surge that reached Pompeii at about 7 in the morning. Then we look at what science has found since: the charcoal inscription that moves the date to autumn, the skeletons on Herculaneum's beach, a brain turned to glass, CT scans of the casts, ancient DNA, and burned scrolls read without being opened.
